Scan the surface for epitopes

You have an antigen but no antibody, so there is no known epitope. Rather than predict one, episcaf runs growPatch, an exhaustive surface-space search: it enumerates every region that could plausibly be an antibody footprint, so you can scaffold them all and let the experiment decide. The first step, seed-and-grow, grows candidate patches outward from exposed surface residues; an antibody’s-eye-view pass adds the footprints a real antibody could cover but growing never reaches.

Use this when: you want broad coverage of a target's surface, or you are building a tiled library and don't want to commit to one site.

Scaffold a known epitope

You already know the epitope — from an antibody–antigen complex, or a chosen set of residues. The scaffolder designs a protein that presents it in its native, antibody-binding conformation, then scores each design on fidelity and accessibility.

Use this when: you have a complex (e.g. a SAbDab entry) or a specific site you want displayed on a stable, synthesizable scaffold.
